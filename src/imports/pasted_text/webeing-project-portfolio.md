## 1. Webeing (위빙)

**Period:** January 2020 – June 2021
**Role:** Co-founder, Product Owner, Software Engineer
**Team:** 2 co-founders, 1 designer, 5 software engineers
**Skills:** Entrepreneurship · Product Management · Django · HTML · CSS · JavaScript · Bootstrap · AWS · Marketing · Sales · Fundraising · Full-Stack Engineering · Business Development · A/B Test · MVP Test · Prototyping · Leadership · Customer Development · Market Research
**GitHub:** [github.com/oereo/Webeing](https://github.com/oereo/Webeing)

### About

> *Q. What is the best way to consume leftover food wisely?*
> *Q. What is the best way to buy food at a reasonable price?*

Webeing was a **B2B2C food e-commerce platform** designed to help restaurants sell near-expiry food and unsold stock at discounted prices — particularly fruit and small dishes. We built it with an environmental mission, encouraging customers to take part in sustainability-focused activities.

- Our mission was to reduce the problem of **excessive food waste** in restaurants through community-driven environmental activities.
- We helped owners of small businesses **sell leftover food** close to its expiration date or **repurpose unsold inventory**, while customers could buy food at a discounted price.
- We were an **ESG-driven startup**, offering features like personal environmental accounts, eco-friendly goods, and community-based crowdfunding to encourage responsible consumption.

### Problems

- South Korea generates over **5.5 million tons of food waste annually**, resulting in an economic loss of over ₩22 trillion (~$16 billion). This is mostly driven by Korea's group dining (회식) and drinking culture.
- **Single-person households are on the rise**, and the market for small-portion convenience meals is growing.
- **The rate of consumption shifted dramatically after COVID-19**, as people became heavily reliant on online food delivery services.

### Market & User Research

- We initially **targeted Wirye New Town, Seongnam City (성남 위례신도시)** for analysis and product development.
- We compiled a list of **50+ restaurant owners** and interviewed them as potential customers.
- We conducted both qualitative and quantitative **customer interviews with over 300 local citizens** in Wirye New Town.
- We regularly worked inside the restaurants themselves to deeply understand their operations and interact with local customers.

**Insights — Restaurant Owners:**
- A key motivation for owners was advertising: our platform gave them a new marketing channel to reach customers.
- Some bars and restaurants were reluctant to openly sell "leftovers," fearing it would signal that business was failing. This was a real UX and communication challenge we had to navigate.

**Insights — Service Users:**
- Customers were frustrated by the high cost of delivery fees and a lack of direct food channels.
- Over-packaging from delivery was a recurring pain point — customers felt guilty about the waste.

### Solutions

- We provided discounted near-expiry food and stock items so owners could minimize loss and customers could pick up items near their homes.
- We helped restaurants increase revenue through inventory monitoring, leftover repurposing, and platform-based advertising.
- Users could engage with environmental activities, buy eco-goods (e.g. eco bags) in-app, and earn rewards through **"Environmental Accounts"** — a financial rewards system for eco-friendly behavior.
- Example campaigns: trash-collection drives, one-day classes to make items from recycled materials.

### Prototypes

We designed 2 types of prototype:
- **Web Service**
- **Hybrid App (Web + Application)**

Tools used: Adobe XD, Protopie

Design principles:
- Intuitive UX to help users quickly recognize discounted items
- Relay the environmental mission visually throughout the product
- Reward repeat users with eco-friendly goods so they could see the tangible impact of their purchases
- Created a Kakao Channel for real-time customer interaction

### Results

- Partnered with **5 restaurants** in the Wirye area.
- Interviewed over **300 potential customers** through deep qualitative and quantitative research.
- Pivoted several times and redesigned prototypes **5 times** before reaching the final product.
- Released a **beta website**, followed by a **new native web-app**.
- Generated a small revenue and **helped partner restaurants increase sales by 30%**.
- Received government investment through the *'Package for Prospective Founders (예비창업자 특화 패키지분야)'* program.
- Won **2nd Prize** at the Lotte Corporation & Likelion Online Hackathon.
- Won **3rd Prize** at the Chung-Ang University Davinci SW.AI Startup Idea Contest.

### What I Learned

- I learned the full lifecycle of building a service from 0 to 1 — from identifying a market problem to meeting customers and shipping a real product.
- Business hypotheses rarely match actual market realities. Meeting customers frequently and listening deeply is non-negotiable.
- The go-to-market strategy of starting in a specific, contained location was one of our most valuable decisions.

## 2. 🎶 Busking Town

**Period:** September 2021 – January 2022
**Role:** Project Leader, Prototype Developer
**Team:** 5 team members
**Skills:** Market Research · Planning · Leadership · Prototyping
**Demo:** [Gather Town Space](https://app.gather.town/app/E5ysIt1kaIrPAxmC/buskingtown_hongdae)

### About

> *Q. Is there any way to visit a concert without going in person and still interact actively?*

Busking Town was a **metaverse-based online busking platform** built to support indie artists and revive the declining live culture around **Hongdae (홍대)** — devastated by COVID-19. We created a virtual space where artists and audiences could meet naturally, interact freely, and enjoy live music without physical barriers.

The space was built inside **Gather Town**, modeled after real Hongdae locations: cafes, clothing stores, clubs, and live stages. Audiences could join through avatars, request songs, chat, and experience the spontaneous energy of street busking — all online.

### Problems

COVID-19 hit the **offline music and performance industry** hard. Indie music and the Hongdae area especially suffered. We wanted to use local resources and digital tools to help recover the ecosystem.

### Solutions

- We created a **metaverse space** that eliminated barriers of physical distance, time, scale, and stage access.
- We embedded real local landmarks — cafes, clubs, performance stages — to preserve the authentic Hongdae atmosphere.
- Artists could access a dedicated stage and interact with fans through **commenting, song requests, donations, and avatar movement**.
- We ran events, created playlists, and designed content to attract users.

**Business Model:**
- Artists could perform live or add their music to our playlist as a new revenue channel.
- We focused on indie artists doing "busking-style" shows — low barrier, real interaction.
- Crowdfunding-style concert access: audiences could discover new artists at affordable prices.

### Results

- Researched the Hongdae market and interviewed around **100 people** involved in or passionate about music.
- Conducted quantitative and qualitative research to validate the hypothesis.
- Successfully held **one online concert event** inside the metaverse.

## 3. ☕ LITER

**Period:** July 2020 – September 2020
**Role:** Project Leader, Prototype Designer
**Team:** 4 team members
**Skills:** Planning · Prototyping · Market Research · Leadership

### About

> *Q. Is there any way to gather all rewards — points, coupons, and more — from all cafes in one integrated place?*

LITER was a proposed **integrated loyalty and rewards platform** for small, non-franchise cafes. We aimed to solve the fragmentation problem: customers juggle different paper stamps and digital points from every independent cafe they visit, while franchise giants like Starbucks offer seamless digital experiences.

Our solution unified the experience — payment, recommendations, mobile vouchers, rewards, and QR-based point collection — into a single app. We even visualized reward points as a filling coffee cup to make progress tangible and fun.

### Problems

Small non-franchise cafes rely on outdated customer acquisition methods — paper coupons, disconnected stamp cards. Customers end up with cluttered wallets and no way to track their collective rewards. Meanwhile, big chains have polished digital loyalty ecosystems that small cafes simply can't compete with.

### Solutions

- A **unified rewards app** where customers could collect and spend points across any participating independent cafe.
- Cafe owners could use the platform as a **marketing and customer acquisition channel**.
- Features included: QR-based payment and rewards collection, mobile gifting, announcements, and remote ordering.
- Unified points meant customers could earn at Cafe A and spend at Cafe B — breaking down loyalty silos.

### Prototype Highlights

- The reward system was visualized as a **filling coffee cup** — intuitive and satisfying.
- Designed for fast information retrieval so users could act quickly without friction.

### Results

- We participated in the **'Try Everything 2020 Idea Contest'** and reached the final stage.

## 4. 🇰🇿 Global Innovator Festa (GIF) Hackathon

**Period:** October 2019 – November 2019
**Role:** Project Leader
**Team:** 9 team members (Korea + Kazakhstan)
**Skills:** Market Research · English · Leadership

### About

> *Q. How can we provide a safer, better public transportation experience in Kazakhstan?*
> *Q. How can we narrow the technological gap between Kazakhstan and Korea?*

This was a **global innovation hackathon** where I led a Korean-Kazakh mixed team to solve the problem of **digital information polarization** — specifically, applying Korean technology to improve the safety and efficiency of Kazakhstan's public transportation system.

### Problems

Public transportation in Kazakhstan was systemically unsafe and underdeveloped:
- Frequent incidents of kidnapping, sexual harassment, and pickpocketing on public transit.
- Many people resorted to hitchhiking due to the lack of reliable alternatives.
- Children regularly went missing in transit.

### Market Research

- Kazakhstan's public transit was not well-regulated or digitized.
- Most people relied on informal transport methods, exposing themselves to dangerous situations daily.

### Solutions

We proposed a system to **track people's location** by saving their latest location data when tapping a public transit card — a lightweight but effective safety layer that could be implemented using existing card infrastructure.

### Result

Our team won the **'Top Global Prize'** as an international team.

### What I Learned

During the hackathon, our Korean team members described Kazakhstan as an "underdeveloped country" that needed Korean technology — a framing that upset our Kazakh teammates. We worked through the conflict and found a respectful, collaborative approach. It taught me that **how you frame a problem matters as much as the solution itself**, especially in cross-cultural settings.

## 5. ✈️ Travel CP (Content Provider)

**Period:** April 2019 – December 2022
**Role:** Project Leader
**Team:** 2 team members
**Skills:** English · Planning · Marketing · Leadership · Market Research · MVP Test

### About

> *Q. How can we provide high-quality, interest-based tourism experiences to foreign visitors by connecting them with local Koreans who share their passions?*

Travel CP was a platform for providing **interest-based travel content and experiences** in Korea, curated by both Korean locals and experienced foreigners as Content Providers (CPs). The idea was that no one understands what a foreigner wants from Korea better than someone who has already lived that experience.

### Problems

Travel information online is scattered and generic. Korea's tourism is a red ocean — tourists are often disappointed because the content doesn't match their actual interests. Additionally, many foreigners who live in Korea want to connect with locals but struggle to break through social barriers.

### Market & User Research

From customer interviews, key insights emerged:
- Foreigners in Korea universally found Koreans friendly but hard to befriend authentically.
- Top interests among foreign residents: traveling around Korea, having real conversations with Koreans, and K-pop or music-related activities.
- Foreigners living in Korea fell into three segments: exchange students, short-term visitors, and long-term employees — each with different needs and communities.

**Memorable Insights:**
- On subway rides, some Koreans visibly avoided sitting next to foreigners.
- In group projects at university, foreign students were often excluded from Korean teams.
- Many Koreans actively approached foreigners to practice English — sometimes making the interaction feel transactional rather than genuine.

We found each group had its own communities: Kakao group chats, Slack workspaces, and Instagram communities.

### Solutions

We organized **interest-based events and experiences**:
- BBQ trips and regional tours around Korea
- Sports events (climbing, football, CrossFit)
- Music and K-pop-themed activities

We also provided **curated travel content** from CPs who understood both Korean culture and the perspective of the visitor — complete with travel routes, local spots, and optional in-person guidance.

The core value: creating a natural environment where Koreans and foreigners could connect around shared interests.

### Results

- Conducted **20+ MVP tests** with customers from the US, Singapore, Vietnam, Mongolia, Bulgaria, Kazakhstan, Ireland, Australia, and more.

### What I Learned

- How to communicate meaningfully across multiple cultures.
- Customer satisfaction spikes when experiences are built around personal interests — and when the people involved are genuinely great company.

## 6. 🇺🇸 International Business Program — North American Market Strategy

**Period:** April 2019 – June 2019
**Role:** Project Leader, Tour Guide
**Team:** 5 team members
**Skills:** Market Research · Marketing · Leadership · English · Global Expansion · Market Strategy
**Presentation:** [Google Slides](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1h8Lk-oWI66OTyV4fzLV2e_I76QF5pTUj9oFLGhBwCic/edit?usp=sharing)

### About

> *Q. What is the best way to help a Korean startup expand its market to the US and North America?*

This was an **intensive field consulting project** focused on developing a global marketing strategy for **Emong Games** — a Korean HTML5 game startup — to break into the North American market.

### Problems

Emong Games had developed an HTML5-based browser game (no download required) but found the Korean market too small to sustain growth. They needed to validate whether there was a real opportunity in North America and understand what adjustments were needed for U.S. users.

### Market Research

We analyzed the **e-sports market in both Korea and the US**. Korea is a global leader in e-sports, but the U.S. game market follows very different trends and consumption patterns. The challenge was translating a Korean product's strengths into a compelling U.S. value proposition.

### Solutions

We recommended combining Emong Games' HTML5 browser game format with **educational content** — a positioning uniquely suited to the U.S. market, where edtech and gamified learning have strong traction.

### Results

- We pitched our strategy directly to **Emong Games executives**.
- They responded positively and indicated they would seriously consider the U.S. market expansion based on our findings.

### What I Learned

- Deep exposure to **international business strategy focused on the U.S. market**.
- Significant insight into the IT and gaming industry from a global perspective.
- Presented research on North American market conditions, competitor analysis, and business model recommendations.

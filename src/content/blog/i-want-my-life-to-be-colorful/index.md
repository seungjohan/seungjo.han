# I Want My Life to Be Colorful

### Breaking free from a fluctuating sense of self-worth, knocking on the doors of the world, and learning to be honest with myself.

*By Seungjo Han · Feb 6, 2026*

---

![Opening | size=wide](/blog-images/i-want-my-life-to-be-colorful_1.jpg)

When it came to asking "questions," I was a kid who never hesitated. When it came to taking on challenges out of curiosity, I didn't care what anyone thought. I simply gave things a try.

I often served as class president or student council president at school. To everyone around me, I was the proactive, sociable, model student. But paradoxically, deep down, I was a completely different person, impatient, full of thoughts and worries, constantly conscious of what others thought of me. In a way, an introverted and sensitive person underneath it all.

When I saw trash in the school hallway, I often picked it up, not because I thought "there's trash in a public place, I should clean it up", but because "I'm the class president, and people are watching." I had gotten so used to viewing my life through other people's eyes that I slowly lost any real opinion of my own, and was so concerned with how I was seen. I became a yes-man, who went with the flow without much of an opinion or any sharp edges, and I began to lose my confidence.

I didn't feel like myself. I wasn't being honest with myself. And at some point, I started to dislike that version of myself.

Still, when it came to things I was curious about, I didn't hesitate to ask questions, and I made a conscious effort to actively explore whatever piqued my curiosity. At school, I was a student who asked a lot of questions. In middle and high school, I visited my math and science teachers' desks so often to ask questions that other teachers would sometimes joke, "Why do you come here so much?" or "Don't you live in the faculty room?" Whenever I didn't understand something, I would immediately grab a teacher and do my best to ask questions and figure it out.

I constantly wanted to dig deeper and learn more. After all, there were so many things in the world I didn't know. I also thought a lot about philosophical matters.

When I entered college and left my hometown to start life in Seoul, I was still a yes-man. I met many new people and wanted to have a successful college experience, so I wanted to make a good impression on everyone. As a result, I often treated people insincerely. Looking back now, it wasn't healthy. I didn't feel like I was actually attending college, and since I wasn't being honest, I often felt that my relationships weren't genuine either.

As my environment changed, I gained diverse experiences, and my horizons and interests broadened, I always ended up back at the same question. Amidst all these changes, how should I move forward?

So I turned inward and asked myself:

> What makes my heart race?
> Have I ever pursued something I loved so much it took my breath away?
> Where am I heading?

So I began tackling the things I love, one by one, to be honest with myself. I wanted to take on challenges for my own sake.

## Travel. The way to discover my most authentic self

I decided to start with the things I love most, the things that make my heart race. I wanted to take on a challenge that would push me past my limits. So I cycled the 660-kilometer national cycling route from Incheon to Busan. Then, two years later, wanting to share this experience, I took my father and younger brother along for another trip, completing a total of two cross-country bike rides.

![Cycling the national bike route | size=wide](/blog-images/i-want-my-life-to-be-colorful_2.jpg)

I never knew our country was this beautiful. As I rode along the rivers and across the vast fields, cutting through the wind, I saw a massive mountain on the distant horizon enveloping the entire landscape, and in that moment, I felt a sense of happiness I hadn't experienced in a very long time. I found myself wondering how many things in my life had ever made me feel so wildly enthusiastic, so happy that my heart was pounding, and I realized that this must be one of those moments. That time became one of the most overwhelming and precious moments of my life, an experience that gave me a great sense of accomplishment for having achieved what I set out to do.

A month before enlisting in the military, I went on a backpacking trip across the country with nothing but a backpack and no plan. I felt overwhelmed by the fact that I had to enlist in a few months, anxious about traveling alone, and nervous about setting off without any plans. But I felt that if I got bogged down in reasons why it wouldn't work, I wouldn't be able to do anything at all. I thought that if I did something, anything, then maybe something would happen, so I just bolted out the door. Although it was a trip I set out on without a plan, I had conversations with all sorts of people and, luckily, received a lot of kind help. I chatted with an elderly woman at a traditional market, talked with an Airbnb host, and even struck up conversations with strangers on the street. Thanks to the stories they shared, I found the courage to move forward with choices I wasn't even sure about myself.

If you look at the Korean word for "beautiful", 아름답다, the root 아름 actually means "I" or "me." In other words, it implies that I am most beautiful when I am being myself.

For me, travel is a moment when I can discover my most honest self, it's when I am most truly myself. It's an important time for me to get to know myself and figure out where I should be heading.

## Dokdo Security Police. A challenge to reclaim my self-esteem

![Dokdo Security Police | size=wide](/blog-images/i-want-my-life-to-be-colorful_3.jpg)

As the time to enlist approached, the Dokdo Security Police was the only option that came to mind. While I had visited the island during a school field trip in middle and high school, I chose the Dokdo Security Police above all else to build my self-esteem and make a choice that truly set my heart racing. During a chaotic period when I wasn't sure what I liked or what kind of life I should live, I wanted to make a choice that would make me proud of myself.

Not every moment of those 21 months was happy. There were many difficult and painful times. However, I achieved significant personal growth, and I was able to gain a wealth of experience in a place that is a source of great pride for Koreans, a place few people can easily visit. I got to know the residents of Ulleung Island, and my heart swelled with warmth as I received encouragement from visitors to Dokdo.

In particular, when I left Ulleung Island and docked at Dokdo for the first time, where my service would officially begin, I looked at Dokdo through the ship's window and could hear nothing but the pounding of my own heart. In that moment, I felt immense pride and passion. It was the moment I resolved to pursue what I love with all my heart for the rest of my life.

Thanks to the Dokdo Security Police, my patriotism grew, but above all, my self-esteem soared, and it was a crucial time that gave me great courage for my life. It became a major turning point that allowed me to transform into a person with my own unique identity, rather than just blending in with others.

## Cold Emails. I wanted to ask as many questions as possible and learn as much as I could

Even when I wanted to do something or learn about it, there was so much I didn't know, and all I could see were my own shortcomings. That's why I had to ask and learn as much as possible. I was so desperate that I didn't even have time to feel embarrassed, I actively reached out to people I wanted to learn from and asked them questions. Thanks to those who applauded my courage and listened to me, I gained the opportunity to see the wider world and found the courage to keep challenging myself.

### With the mindset of "fall nine times, get up ten."

Once I became interested in startups, I wanted to really dig deep into them. So I tried to attend every event that covered helpful topics, networking parties, conferences, and information sessions, whatever came my way. There were many times I came home feeling timid and unable to say a word, but that's how I gradually began to learn about startups.

While searching for structured startup education, I discovered an institution planning a systematic, year-long global startup training program. Just as I was excitedly filling out the application, I received the disappointing news that the program had been postponed. Feeling desperate not to lose the opportunity, I scoured the internet to find the founder's email address and reached out. No reply came.

However, I was too eager to give up, so I started sending follow-up emails to make my case. It was around my 8th or 10th email, perhaps my sincerity got through, because the founder replied, inviting me to the office. But even that encounter lasted only about three minutes squeezed in between other meetings, and I had no choice but to leave with just a business card and the disappointing news that the program had been postponed indefinitely. This wasn't the meeting I had hoped for.

Well, I had sent 10 emails, and now I had a business card, wasn't that a step forward? I tried to console myself with that thought, and as I left the office, I immediately sent the founder a text message. I told them I wanted to meet again. Fortunately, they suggested I come by one more time that week. So this time, I decided to prepare a presentation to make a strong impression in that short window and really solidify my pitch. At our second meeting, I got straight to the point as soon as I arrived. Perhaps they appreciated my passion, because halfway through my presentation, they unexpectedly made a new proposal, asking if I'd like to help with a startup and digital nomad program they were preparing in Jeju. That's how I ended up receiving an opportunity I never could have imagined.

![Jeju startup and digital nomad program | size=wide](/blog-images/i-want-my-life-to-be-colorful_4.jpg)

Because I was so desperate, I knocked on that door with the mindset of "fall nine times, get up on the tenth", and that persistence created the opportunity. That experience gave me the confidence and belief that anything is possible if you take on a challenge with conviction. Through that program in Jeju, I met people from various professions and numerous startups, which allowed me to learn about the startup founding process firsthand and gain insights I hadn't known before. As my worldview expanded, my heart swelled once again with the courage to believe I could succeed.

### The courage to get back up after rejection

After that time in Jeju, I developed an interest in early-stage startups and coworking spaces. While browsing articles and researching businesses, I came across a story featuring the co-founder of a global coworking space company. I searched for their contact information online and reached out, and was grateful to have the opportunity to meet with them during their lunch break.

They asked me why I wanted to start a company, shared their own story, and sincerely offered advice from my perspective. Then they added:

> Actually, earlier at lunch, I was eating with a colleague and mentioned I had a meeting with you. My colleague asked, 'Why bother meeting and helping a student you don't even know? Isn't that a waste of time?' But honestly, I don't see it that way. I, too, was once like you, a stranger, full of passion, and in need of help. After reading your passionate message, I made time because I wanted to. I hope you found this time valuable, and I hope my advice was helpful. I'll be cheering for your challenge.

Their sincere words made my heart race, and I felt a bit dazed. In fact, this was the first time I had indirectly received a negative reaction to my dream regarding cold emails. While I reflected deeply on how grateful I am that a complete stranger would help another without expecting anything in return, I also realized that since it's only natural not to receive help, resilience, the ability to brush off rejection and get back up, is crucial.

Facing countless rejections is a natural part of taking on challenges. That's exactly why we must keep challenging ourselves, asking questions, and learning along the way. Feeling even more grateful for every helping hand I received, I used that energy as fuel to keep going.

## Startups. My message to the world and my challenge

If someone asked me why I decided to start a startup, I would answer like this:

> It's because I want to take on the challenge of using my own vision to solve the world's problems and make a positive impact on people through the things I love.

In this rapidly changing world, I wanted to take on innovative challenges to proactively solve the world's problems, not as a mere follower, but as a pioneer. My heart raced at the thought of staying ahead of the times, using cutting-edge technology to deliver new experiences and impact to people, and creating positive change in the world. I felt that if I could convey my vision and message through this challenge, it would be a truly fulfilling endeavor.

### Street vending

I knew I was still full of uncertainties, but I felt I would regret it if I started looking for reasons why it wouldn't work and never even got started. So I decided to find whatever I could do and put my ideas into action as quickly as possible.

Coincidentally, I had the opportunity to learn about the difference between blue oceans and red oceans at school. Just reading about it didn't really resonate with me, I vaguely thought that since there were no competitors, a blue ocean must be unconditionally good. So I decided I wanted to understand it by taking on the challenge myself.

I chose street vending. Creating a workflow to produce and sell products with my own funds was a daunting challenge, but it was a hurdle I was determined to overcome. After searching for a market in Korea that was a blue ocean with plenty of friction to test, I chose the adult products market. Although it is culturally and psychologically conservative and has almost no competitors, I judged it to be a space our society needs to move through to become healthier, so I felt it was worth the challenge.

Above all, the most important concept for me was creating a space where people could visit without feeling any pressure. I designed the booth to feel light and approachable, like a casual date spot, and decorated the products with cute, festive designs for the Christmas season to minimize psychological distance as much as possible. To create an art exhibition-like atmosphere, I hung various playful artistic photographs on the walls. When customizing products, I turned cute or funny photos into stickers and packaged them in red and green to position them as charming gift items.

Although I launched this as a pop-up event for the Christmas season, it ultimately failed, we were unable to properly sell our products due to issues with the booth quality, product reliability, and a lack of understanding of customer flow. However, by planning and preparing everything myself, I was able to experience and learn much more directly than I ever could have just by thinking about it.

The most profound lesson I learned firsthand is that there is a world of difference between merely conceiving an idea and actually putting that idea into action. Taking action means you have gone out to understand what customers want and have continuously explored the problem. It goes beyond simply having an idea, it means understanding that cycle and actively exploring solutions by facing challenges head-on. The difference is enormous.

A product creator must know how to build persuasiveness by meticulously planning and designing even the smallest details that consumers might overlook. In a blue ocean, a market where the structure hasn't fully formed, there are more complex hurdles to overcome when persuading consumers. You have to convince them not just to buy your product, but also explain why they need it, what value it offers, and how it delivers that value.

I sought feedback from people around me both before and after taking on this challenge. Among the reactions I received, the most disappointing were:

> "It wasn't going to work anyway, so why bother?" "Isn't this just a challenge for the sake of a challenge?" "Shouldn't you stop doing this and just steadily build up your credentials like everyone else?"

To be honest, they weren't wrong. I knew it probably wouldn't work out anyway. It was a lesson I could have learned without even trying. But I believe that if you start by looking for reasons why it won't work, you'll never attempt anything at all. Instead of focusing on why it won't work, ask how you can succeed, what you can learn to get closer to a solution, and how that attempt might lead you somewhere you never expected. Even if someone else has already proven it won't work, I believe that if the situation is different, the method is different, and I am different, I can gain a different kind of insight. Only by continuously breaking through those walls can I transcend my limits and grow. There is inevitably a huge difference, both in terms of learning and mindset, between actually trying something and not trying it at all.

That's how I asked questions, learned, stumbled through trial and error, failed, and learned all over again.

### Startup: Webeing

I wanted to use software to solve a problem I had personally experienced and was deeply interested in. I believed that if I could make an impact on people and create positive change through this, it would be a challenge worth throwing myself into with a full sense of responsibility.

So I began to observe everything within my sphere of life, from daily routines to the conversations and behaviors of those around me, one by one. As I reflected on the lens through which I had been viewing the world, I took a fresh look at myself. While searching for ideas, asking what vision I wanted to convey through the problem I sought to solve and how passionately I could commit to solving it, I asked myself countless questions.

![Webeing startup work | size=wide](/blog-images/i-want-my-life-to-be-colorful_5.jpg)

Since I lived near a university campus, I would often walk through the neighborhood in the early morning or late at night and easily spot rows of trash lined up along the streets. This was a regrettable environmental issue stemming directly from Korea's drinking culture and college students' excessive consumption, all leading to food waste. As someone who felt this problem personally every day, I wanted to use solving it as an opportunity to foster a shift toward a healthier food culture. That's why I decided to take on the challenge of creating a service that offers products nearing their expiration dates or excess inventory to consumers at affordable prices.

I compiled a list of bustling districts in Seoul and began surveying them one by one. I met with various small business owners, from cafes to bars and restaurants, to hear about their struggles. I sought to understand all aspects of the food service industry, from food waste to profit margins and logistics. Since I had never run a restaurant myself, I focused even more intently on the customers and made a concerted effort to understand them. To be honest, in the beginning we were rather arrogant, we tried to move forward based solely on the hypotheses we had come up with ourselves, presuming we could sufficiently anticipate consumers' problems and needs. Sure enough, when we actually conducted interviews, we heard stories from business owners that were completely different from our initial hypotheses.

Most small businesses don't have a distinct marketing strategy; they simply try to increase the number of marketing channels they use, since more exposure is highly likely to lead to higher sales. Small business owners were therefore less interested in our main service concept and more interested in using our platform as just another marketing channel. If they sold inventory nearing expiration through our service, consumers might perceive this as evidence of poor store performance, a potential risk to their brand image in the long run. This was particularly true for the bar industry, where people tend to flock to popular spots. Rather than selling excess inventory, offering it as a complimentary service to existing customers was said to directly lead to repeat business and higher sales.

When going to restaurants for meals, buying coffee, or distributing flyers, I spent a great deal of time with local residents and made every effort to interview potential customers. Thanks to this, I was able to gain a deep understanding of the situations and needs of a diverse range of people.

Before creating the product, during development, and even afterward, I continued to expand my customer base and consistently met with customers. In particular, I formed a close partnership with a fruit vendor, together we attempted to develop new products, created pamphlets to promote their store, and even helped out with operations during the Chuseok holiday rush. By getting out there and rolling up my sleeves, I gained a deep understanding of logistics, ordering, inventory management, sales, and marketing, and quickly became familiar with the food service industry as a whole.

We worked with our partners to continuously test our service and ideas in the market, pivoting as needed in our quest to find Product-Market Fit (PMF). We constantly explored various approaches beyond the solution we initially proposed, always asking how we could genuinely address what our customers actually needed. Although we helped boost our partners' sales and generated some revenue ourselves, we were unable to find a solution or PMF that could encompass the broad customer segment of small business owners, and we regrettably had to shut down the service.

It was disappointing, but I learned a great deal.

I learned that we must create a service that is of the customer, by the customer, and for the customer. It must not be based solely on my own assumptions or what I want to convey, but rather on a deep understanding of the customer gained through active engagement, identifying their real pain points and building something that truly meets their needs.

To solve problems I had personally experienced through software, I continuously conducted market research and customer development in various ways. I brainstormed ideas, tested them in the market, and collaborated with customers. Through these diverse and intense efforts, I grew into a person with strong execution ability, and, surprisingly, it became an opportunity to learn more about myself.

### After going through numerous trials and failures

I have long wanted to take on the challenge of anticipating the trends of the times and staying ahead of them. That's why I think deeply about the current trends of this era and how they will evolve.

The defining theme of our current era is undoubtedly AI. It is no exaggeration to say that AI is completely transforming the world. Looking beyond AI agents to AGI and the application of AI across various industries, I believe the opportunities are limitless and that anything we can imagine is possible.

To be there, I believe we must interpret the world from diverse perspectives through a broad range of interests, and be willing to keep taking on challenges. Even if I fail, I want to get back up and continue challenging myself with a constant sense of curiosity about the world.

## Languages. A new perspective on the world, deeper understanding, and a door to new opportunities

I believe that language is not merely a means of communication, but rather a framework for viewing the world that offers new perspectives and understanding. Above all, language provides the opportunity to communicate directly with others, understand our differences, and share our unique experiences and ways of thinking.

English is already so important that I practice it daily, as naturally as eating a meal. Thanks to this, I've truly felt that the range of opportunities available to me has expanded. I'm now taking it a step further by studying Spanish and French as well.

There's a saying that the best way to understand a country's culture is to learn its language. While English allowed me to learn about the pioneering spirit of American culture and the global stage, studying Spanish and French has helped me expand my horizons into various fields such as world history, music, and art. Language embodies a country's background, way of life, culture, and perspectives. I could truly feel firsthand that there are ways of life different from those in Korea.

![Studying languages | size=wide](/blog-images/i-want-my-life-to-be-colorful_6.jpg)

One interesting experience: when I encountered the number sept while studying French, it looked so much like the word "September," which I already knew. This curiosity linked sept and September in my mind, and I naturally came to realize that this connection extended all the way to world history, starting with Julius Caesar, as well as the historical trends of religion, art, and music. For someone like me, who had absolutely no interest in history during my school days, this awakened a completely different approach compared to when I mindlessly memorized "September" as the ninth month.

In Korea, we often comment on appearance as a way of showing interest, "Oh, you've lost weight," or "Why do you look so tired today?" In many other countries, commenting on someone's appearance is considered quite rude. In places like Europe or the United States, where people from different cultural backgrounds naturally mix and interact, respect and understanding for individual diversity seem much deeper and more natural. Conversely, although Korea is geographically a peninsula, it can feel like an island in terms of cultural exposure, we are still in the early stages of learning about diversity.

As the saying goes, "You have to listen to the end of a Korean sentence", Korea's Confucian culture and emphasis on politeness often lead people to save the main point for the end or speak indirectly. In contrast, in Western countries, failing to convey your opinion clearly is often seen as a lack of confidence. Language is a mirror that reflects ways of thinking and culture.

As I became aware of these differences in perspective, I began to truly accept diversity, realizing that what I had always known wasn't necessarily the only correct answer. I naturally developed an open mind, becoming more willing to listen to opinions different from my own and to accept my own shortcomings with the same openness. Having the flexibility to view the world from diverse perspectives has helped me connect the dots between my various experiences and expanded how I think. It has made me a more well-rounded person, and it serves as a vital foundation for my growth. That is why I will continue to keep language close at hand, take an interest in diverse cultures and perspectives, and strive to remain an open-minded person.

## Paying it forward. The kindness I want to share with the world

### Small talk. That seemingly nosy kindness is actually the kindness that brightens my life

Since I was born and raised in Korea, small talk isn't a particularly natural part of our culture. In Korea, it's very common to be taken aback if a stranger suddenly greets you. However, I tend to approach strangers, especially foreigners, in a friendly manner, greeting them or trying to make small talk. I tend to be the type to step forward to help or strike up a conversation first. It might seem nosy or even a bit pushy, but I believe that those small smiles and seemingly intrusive acts of kindness can make my day, and my life, more positive. And if small talk leads to one more reason to smile, that's a bonus.

I feel especially happy when I think that my small acts of consideration can mean a lot to foreigners visiting Korea, and that they can help leave them with a warm impression of this country.

I believe that the kindness of greeting people warmly is ultimately the kindness that brightens my own life too. Since the world reflects back to me exactly how I choose to see and act, I strive to make my life a positive one.

### Kindness without expectation. Passing on the kindness received from a stranger to yet another stranger

Once, while traveling in Gangwon Province, I stayed at an Airbnb. The host treated me warmly even though I was disheveled from wandering around all day, and listened intently to my travel stories. Then they suggested I check out a nearby ski resort, saying they had an acquaintance working there who could get me a free lift ticket. Only two buses ran through that area each day, and the last one left at 1:30 PM, meaning I could get in, but there was no obvious way back out. Still, with the mindset that it would work out somehow, I accepted the offer.

Sure enough, after skiing, I had no way to get back to the city. Determined to at least try hitchhiking, I started at the entrance to the ski resort and walked along the road toward the city for about two hours, asking people for help. Some people didn't even respond when I spoke to them. Feeling discouraged, I kept walking, but fortunately, just as I reached the final stretch leading to the national highway, a truck driver pulled over and gave me a ride. During the short drive, we chatted and laughed, and I arrived safely at a nearby town.

Afterward, as I reflected on the experience, I kept thinking about the people who had shown me such undeserved kindness. How could they be so kind to a complete stranger without expecting anything in return? And how could I ever repay them?

After much thought, the answer I arrived at was this: I pass it on, to other strangers, unconditionally.

I believe that if I treat people the way I would like to be treated, my surroundings will eventually be filled with warm and energetic people. And I believe the whole world will gradually become a warmer place. I believe that is my role.

## Music. To reclaim the childhood dream I had lost

At some point, I realized that the dreams I had as a child were fading away as they collided with reality.

- I want to explore all seven wonders of the world.
- I want to become a doctor and treat everyone who is sick.
- I want to explore space.
- I want to become successful and give freely to people, so that everyone can be happy.

I spent my childhood carrying grand dreams, sometimes absurd ones, sometimes ones that simply made me excited just thinking about them. Music was one of them. I loved listening to music, and since I had played the piano for about seven years, I also had the desire to try creating music myself. However, I believed that music was a realm reserved only for those born with natural talent. I thought that art and creativity were far removed from me and would not help my career. So I stopped playing the piano, and my interest in music gradually faded away.

![Music and piano | size=wide](/blog-images/i-want-my-life-to-be-colorful_7.jpg)

As time passed, I briefly played in a band during college and frequently encountered music while attending campus festivals, which gradually reignited my interest. I wanted to express my thoughts and personal experiences more creatively, and convey a message through music. I also felt a sense of regret, wondering why my past self had placed so many limits on my own dreams and underestimated myself.

Above all, the decisive trigger was a friend who gave me a recording microphone as a gift. Although I knew nothing about music production or composition, I just dove in and started by recording covers, whatever I could manage. Thanks to a friend who is skilled at singing and building harmonies, who believed in my dream and helped me along the way, I gained the confidence to give it a try. So I went back to studying piano and delved into various fields like harmony, composition, MIDI software, and orchestration, gradually bringing to life the dreams of composing and working with orchestras that I'd held since childhood. Sounds I had never heard before began to emerge in the music, and I started to get genuinely excited about building harmonies with each new note. Thanks to this, my life has become richer and more vibrant, and I've started to notice things I hadn't seen before, including the artistic layer of the world around me.

I don't want to live a dull, unfulfilled life, overwhelmed by the demands of daily life. Instead, I want to live a vibrant life filled with curiosity, constantly digging deeper, taking on challenges, and interacting with diverse people. I want to live a life where I can encourage myself, saying "you can step outside the fence," and believe in my own possibilities. I want my life to be filled with curiosity.

## I want to remain a person who continues to dream

As time goes on, it's only natural that I have to make choices and focus my energy. As I live my life, opportunities for new experiences or for my passion to reignite naturally become fewer. Before I know it, I can find myself becoming numb to the world I live in and losing sight of its meaning.

But I want to be someone who looks at the world with a soft, flexible curiosity, someone who can knock on the world's door and take on challenges, someone who dreams, and then makes those dreams come true. Rather than looking for reasons why something won't work, I want to be someone who finds ways to make it happen and knows how to put those dreams into action.

Because I am happiest when I'm dreaming. And when I'm making those dreams come true.

That's why I want my life to be colorful.

To paint a colorful life, I will continue to dream many different dreams, and I will take on challenges to make them a reality.

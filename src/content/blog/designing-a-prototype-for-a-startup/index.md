# Designing a Prototype for a Startup to turn your Ideas into Reality

### Prototyping: to transform abstract ideas into tangible, realizable concepts.

*By Seungjo Han · Sep 26, 2024*

If you're curious about design and prototyping, you're likely already considering or working on product development for a project or startup. You might be eager to visualize your ideas. Before diving deeper, it's important to reflect on these questions:

> *How much do you know about prototypes? How critical do you think prototypes are?*

As a writer and ex-founder of a startup, I initially underestimated the importance of prototypes when brainstorming ideas and creating pitch materials. However, I now realize that developing pitch materials without a prototype is almost ineffectual due to the prototype's crucial role.

Prototypes are an essential component when launching a service. They are arguably the most crucial step in the design process.

Just as a building requires a blueprint before construction, or food needs a recipe before cooking, developing an app or website demands a specific and strategic plan — a prototype serves this exact purpose.

> Prototype = Blueprint

Without a well-established prototype, you risk encountering numerous challenges and inefficiencies, akin to navigating a maze without a map. Today, I will share my experiences and insights on the significance of prototypes in the development process.

---

## What is a Prototype?

A prototype is a simulation or sample version of the final product used for testing before launch. The primary purpose of creating a prototype is to test a product or idea before investing significant time and money into its final development.

Prototyping is essential for identifying and resolving usability issues and highlighting areas that need improvement. Once the draft prototype is in your users' hands, you'll discover if they actually want to use it, and if the core features satisfy user needs. Based on their feedback, you can validate your hypotheses, analyze tests, and modify or improve your products. This allows you to adjust and refine your product, and facilitate smoother development.

## The Role of Prototyping

### Visualization enhances persuasiveness and clarifies ideas

Simply having an idea doesn't immediately guarantee the realization of your vision. A prototype is essential because it transforms ideas into tangible products — whether in the form of websites, apps, or other mediums — that connect with users. During this process, visualization helps clarify how the idea will be conveyed. This not only solidifies your concept but also makes your ideas more compelling to present. Visualization turns abstract ideas into concrete, persuasive tools that can effectively engage both users and stakeholders.

Which is more persuasive to you — a recipe for a hotdog or a picture of one?

### Prototyping for rapid testing and decision-making

It's natural to encounter challenges and setbacks when launching a service, but these experiences are valuable learning opportunities. Often, the hypothesized image may not align with reality once the product reaches the market. Through prototyping, one can transform ideas into real features, and create countless beta iterations of the product. This process is called **MVP (minimum viable product) testing**. Through MVP tests, we can gather reactions and feedback from potential customers, general users, and loyal customers, identifying weaknesses and new insights. This iterative process of refining and pivoting strategy is crucial for finding the **product-market fit (PMF)** and tailoring the product to better meet market and customer needs. Even if the design isn't totally perfect, or if auxiliary features are lacking, if you focus on building the core features, it will allow for effective market testing and data collection. This is key to identifying the product market fit.

### Encouraging critical thinking to facilitate ideation

The differences between thought and action can seem small but are actually much more significant. As we develop an idea into a product, unexpected challenges can arise. This prompts us to think critically about how to present solutions to end-users in the most simple and effective way. This process naturally leads to richer and more intuitive ideation, helping to enhance the idea deeply. From personal experience, engaging in this hands-on process of thinking critically not only provides invaluable insights but also reinforces the importance of turning your thoughts into real actions. Thus, I strongly encourage readers to take action. Although the initial attempts might feel awkward, with practice, you'll find yourself becoming more comfortable with the process, and you'll notice a significant improvement in your skills.

## Choosing the Right Prototyping Tool for You

There are numerous prototyping design tools, and new tools are being launched all the time. Some tools are easy to access and contain simple features, while some are more flexible but require more time to adapt to their systems. Selecting the most efficient tool for your project can be challenging.

![Prototyping tools overview by Prototypr.io](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!SxO7!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb421eec2-0417-46ed-91f1-76c4b7de586c_1414x818.heic)

*Photo by [Prototypr.io](https://prototypr.io/)*

Your choice depends on your project's purpose and the level of complexity you wish to implement.

One approach, as shared by a designer friend of mine, involves using three different tools for various stages of prototyping:

> Basic Structure Development → Advanced and Natural Implementation → Adding Complex Variables and Features

Here's a brief overview of the tools we use in my team:

### Paper Prototyping

![Paper prototype made during a class](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!dbjX!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa2713a22-c2aa-4a3c-adbd-d1e4a066a354_1830x1264.png)

*Paper prototype made during a class*

Ideal for those unfamiliar with design tools, but who want to visualize their ideas quickly. If you want to make a simple version of the framework, paper prototyping can be a good option. I also once created a paper prototype. At that time, I didn't know exactly where to start, so I just created simple drawings for a rough layout. In general, I feel that paper prototyping cannot be used as a substitute for more sophisticated tools, however, it is definitely better than using no tools at all.

### Adobe XD

Useful for creating basic structures. It's beginner-friendly, and tutorials are readily available online, so you can self-study in a short period of time. It's excellent for simple functionalities, such as button clicks and transitions, although it may not be sufficient for more complex implementations and is not commonly used in professional environments. Despite these disadvantages, Adobe XD is still useful for the first steps of designing a prototype. Since it is simple and basic, I also used this tool for my first draft prototype.

![Prototype of side project made by Adobe XD](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!dv2E!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe2fd1582-b2c0-41e8-adfd-b167ac3683ac_1772x1014.heic)

*Prototype of side project made by Adobe XD*

### Protopie

Allows for variable assignment, making it suitable for more detailed and interactive prototypes. If you've already used Adobe XD, adapting to Protopie will be straightforward, as it builds on similar design principles.

### Figma

This is the most popular tool currently. Notable for its collaborative features, this web-based program allows online real-time work to be accomplished. All of the UX/UI design is available to be completed within this tool at the same time. It is intuitive and has low hurdles due to its relative ease of use. Recently, Figma underwent a major update by adjusting many of its AI features so that it's more convenient for users' design processes.

I wanted to **make the user experience more familiar** for our consumers. To achieve this, I modeled our designs based on the aesthetics of existing applications, which users already have experience with. I particularly focused on the framework of apps on the iPhone and created a similar design.

This approach is common among designers and developers who benchmark existing services to create a user-friendly UI/UX. For beginners, I recommend starting by benchmarking designs.

## 'Webeing' — A Startup I Founded

I founded a startup, Webeing.

Webeing was an e-commerce-based F&B service that sold leftover food items close to expiration dates, or food inventory at discounted prices, in order to solve Korean food waste and delivery problems while promoting environmental sustainability.

![The logo of 'Webeing'](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!tzcW!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F3ceaf4fb-4a32-4b81-a473-07816622dab4_2000x2000.heic)

*The logo of 'Webeing'*

In Korea, restaurants generate **a significant amount of food waste** because of our food culture. This food waste can be created by multiple reasons, but mainly due to:

- An overabundance of side dishes and soup (typically, many side dishes are provided, free of charge)
- Our drinking culture causing over-ordering of snacks to eat while drinking (known as 'Anju, 안주')

Food waste causes **environmental issues and decreases the income of restaurants**. So, solving these problems was the basis of our startup's objectives. I noticed this issue in the downtown area of my university, which I frequented regularly. Thus, I decided to challenge this problem, as it was located in an area near me, and I felt the necessity to make a positive change in the world. To do so, we needed a product. And to create a better product, we needed to develop prototypes.

After making several iterations of prototypes, we wanted to know if our product would actually work in the market, or if our customer would even want to use it. While building our product, we focused on creating a service that was truly desired by restaurant owners.

My team and I initially considered a service that helps restaurants sell discounted leftovers at closing time and users pick those up, similar to practices in America, Australia, and Europe. This would also enable them to generate secondary income, and to create a new channel of acquisition by allowing people to taste their food. However, through interviews conducted with multiple restaurant owners, it was revealed that most were not in favor of this idea. They were primarily concerned that they could damage their long-term brand reputations by showing customers the amount of leftovers remaining unsold at the end of the day.

Therefore, we proposed additional solutions as well, such as processing leftovers into secondary products like jam and fruit cups, or arranging bulk purchases from farms with a centralized pick-up location.

This led to many discussions during the design and software development stages, where we concentrated on the decisions that truly addressed user needs. Through numerous trials and errors, we tried to incorporate as many overlooked aspects as possible while striving to create a user-focused service.

When developing the product, we asked ourselves multiple questions:

- How to best optimize the user experience?
- What obstacles might appear that could prevent us from meeting user needs?
- Is our chosen problem-solving approach the most efficient?
- Is there an umbrella solution that could generalize the needs of small business owners with diverse issues?
- What are the genuine reasons for users to feel the necessity of our product, and which things are we delivering to them?

Instead of doing paperwork only, I concentrated on **conducting interviews** with as many different types of people as possible, even helping out at their shops while running the service, to better understand their needs. I tried to reflect on these insights when developing the prototype. By listening to their stories and hearing about their real needs, which often differed from what I had originally expected, I worked hard to create a product that truly satisfied their requirements. After decisions and revisions, the product I envisioned was finally born.

### \<4th Prototype Iteration — Protopie\>

We **transitioned to using Protopie** for the final version of our prototype. As mentioned previously, this tool allows for variable assignment, reducing the need to create separate webpages individually, and bringing the prototype closer to the actual service. We could now also incorporate thematic colors, aiming for a more polished design. This iteration included features such as a sliding image format for the landing page, demonstrating dynamic content capabilities.

![Landing page with a slogan and brief introduction before entering our service](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!6Yi5!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fd732c730-bebe-499c-a673-3bce090444ce_1576x1096.png)

*Landing page with a slogan and brief introduction before entering our service*

Still, there remains an ongoing debate about the necessity of a landing page. The landing page serves as the introduction of our company to consumers before using our service. However, it also creates an additional step for users before they can access the core features, potentially causing inconvenience. Besides, users realistically don't often care about a company's vision, instead caring more about their own needs.

**Regardless, our vision was important to me, and I valued it a lot, and truly wanted to encourage and share this with our consumers.** So, we chose to include the landing page.

![Left: home page, Right: inventory detail page](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!iczK!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F50a9e3e2-c549-4dca-aadf-8b1ba8c788e5_3199x1096.png)

*Left: home page, Right: inventory detail page*

From some user opinions received, we found that our UI was not optimal. The primary requirement is that the service should be immediately understandable, and the main intention of our users is that they typically visit the website intending to order food. **On the main page, our restaurants were categorized first, followed by menu items, and more detailed information.** Therefore, displaying the restaurants, rather than the food, may not have aligned with all user expectations.

However, from an engineering perspective, categorizing by location seemed necessary. This was especially important when considering webpages where a single restaurant might have had more than 10 menu items, which could have dominated the main menu display if not organized by establishment. So, I categorized by restaurants. Besides, our service is based on users collecting the food from the restaurants, so we hypothesized that the users would think that the location was as important, or even more so, than that of the menu.

### \<5th Prototype Iteration — final hybrid web-app product\>

After releasing the 4th iteration of the prototype (the beta version), **we received a lot of feedback.** Most was about the necessity of creating an application, and how our landing page was more of a burden to our site visitors than we had previously thought it would be.

Our service had two types of customers: general service users who ordered food, and restaurant owners who used our service as a channel to make additional revenue and for marketing purposes. 'Webeing' centered around our general service users picking up the food from the restaurants themselves. As such, an easily accessible app was far more useful for them, as opposed to using a website.

The purpose of showing the landing page before reaching the main page was to deliver our vision and to encourage our users to join together with us in our vision. However, customers are never as interested as we expect. They have a clear purpose for entering our service. So, the existence of a landing page can actually make customer churn happen. Finally, we made a decision to remove the landing page, and instead to naturally demonstrate our vision to our consumers through a smooth and persuasive product experience.

So based on the previous iterations of our prototypes, we made a final hybrid product of 'Webeing', through collaboration with hybrid web-app developers.

## The Bottom Line

Proposing a solution to a social problem and persuading others of my vision proved far more challenging and complex than I had imagined during the ideation stage. It's easy to be imaginative during that stage, however, it is totally different from the action stage. The more you take a deep dive into a specific topic, the more likely it is that the imagined scenarios will not hold up.

Especially, once you launch your product, you may realize that **the market holds many more challenges for you** than you had expected. It is then also harder to find out what your potential consumers actually require. So, we have to repeat the process of validating the hypothesis like an 'Agile methodology'. This is repeating the process of **'Plan — Design — Develop — Test — Deploy — Review'** until you find out the product market fit and the most advantageous next steps for your startup.

Therefore, **the prototype must continuously be revised and adapted based on user feedback and market change.** Sometimes, it might need to be completely overhauled. This is why having a prototype to set the direction is so important.

It was the same for 'Webeing'. Through developing multiple iterations of the prototypes, we took a look at user reactions and tried to reflect on those. We persisted in trying to find the midpoint between our core values and what our customers wanted.

It is definitely not an easy thing, but it must be done. With the prototype, you must communicate with users, and through trial and error, you can make progress, even if it's just a small step. Unless you take action, an idea remains just an idea. Only by **doing** can you know, and only by **doing** can you achieve something.

Like Nike's slogan says: **Just Do It!**
